## Work Completed

### Headgear Entered Production Organization

The Tron Ares pilot and September 12 recovery point had established the safety model. September 14 applied that model to production Headgear work.

The working process operated on complete candidate packages rather than simply moving files based on filenames or the collection-wide duplicate report.

For each production candidate, the process was designed to:

1. identify the source package and relevant archives;
2. inspect the complete package contents;
3. compare duplicate extracted packages where applicable;
4. compare ZIP payloads with extracted material rather than relying on archive names alone;
5. preserve distinct designs or materially different packages;
6. copy the verified package into the organized Headgear library without altering the source;
7. verify copied content using SHA-256 fingerprints;
8. retain instructions, images, alternate parts, revisions, and other package material with the model;
9. apply item-level metadata where supported;
10. record source, archive, destination, verification, and package relationships; and
11. send uncertainty to review rather than guessing.

The production method continued the established rule that identical individual files did **not** independently authorize consolidation of otherwise distinct packages.

### ZIP Reconciliation Continued Alongside Extracted Packages

ZIP archives were processed as part of the same package-level workflow.

Archives were evaluated by their payloads rather than merely by filenames or the fact that they had been identified during the earlier ZIP inventory.

Where a ZIP matched an already extracted package, the relationship could be documented without creating another permanent browsing copy.

Where a ZIP represented a usable Headgear package not already present in extracted form, its contents could be incorporated into the organized library after verification.

Where an archive contained materially different components, those differences were preserved.

Archives that could not yet be confidently classified remained unresolved rather than being forced into Headgear.

The September 14 record includes explicit examples of this uncertainty handling. A Batman/Predator archive was held for review because generic component names did not adequately establish helmet identity. A Darth Maul/Iron Man archive also required further assessment because its larger package contents could not safely be classified as helmet-only from naming alone. A complete Iron Man suit archive was deferred to Armor rather than broken apart simply to extract its helmet.

### Package Integrity and Provenance Preserved

## Important Questions and Discussions

### What Constituted a Duplicate During Production?

The earlier collection-wide fingerprinting results remained useful for identifying exact-content relationships, but September 14 production work continued to distinguish **file identity from package identity**.

Matching hashes could establish that two individual files were identical.

They could not establish that:

- two complete packages were interchangeable;
- an alternate version was unnecessary;
- one creator's package could replace another's;
- a helmet bundled with a complete armor kit should be separated from that kit; or
- supporting package contents could be discarded.

Package-level comparison remained necessary before consolidation.

### How Should ZIP Archives Be Handled?

ZIPs were not treated as disposable simply because an extracted model existed, nor were they all extracted blindly into the finished library.

Their payloads were reconciled against existing packages.

The desired production result remained **one usable complete package per distinct design/version**, while archive relationships and provenance were recorded separately.

### What Happened When Classification Was Uncertain?

Uncertainty remained an explicit state.

If names, package structure, or available evidence could not establish that something belonged in Headgear, the item was held for review.

Likewise, a package containing broader armor components was deferred to Armor rather than fragmented for the convenience of finishing Headgear.

This prevented the Headgear project from quietly creating damage for the next lifecycle stage—a surprisingly advanced concept for a folder-cleaning project, but here we are.
