## AJ's Objective

Following ARCHIE's initial operational launch, AJ continued refining how different kinds of physical and hobby work should be separated within the project.

Detailed prop-fabrication and finishing work required more sustained execution context than was appropriate for ARCHIE HQ or the broader garage/workshop workstream.

The objective was to create a dedicated environment for that fabrication activity without expanding ARCHIE into unnecessary workstreams for every individual build.

## Work Completed

A dedicated workstream was established:

**LAB — Prop Fabrication & Finishing**

LAB provided a more focused context for fabrication and finishing work while remaining part of the broader ARCHIE operating environment.

A subordinate **RUN** pattern was also established for individual fabrication jobs.

A recoverable example is:

`RUN — Captain America Shield Finish`

This created a structural distinction between the continuing fabrication domain and individual pieces of work performed within it.

## Important Questions and Discussions

The structural problem was similar to the one addressed during ARCHIE's September 4 initialization: persistent work needed enough context to remain manageable without creating unnecessary fragmentation.

Fabrication presented a specific version of that problem.

Treating every prop build as an independent top-level ARCHIE workstream would create excessive structure.

Keeping all fabrication execution inside general ARCHIE operations would make detailed build state difficult to isolate and continue.

The LAB/RUN structure provided an intermediate level:

**ARCHIE → LAB → individual RUN**

The available September 5 evidence supports this structural relationship. It does not support reconstructing a more detailed formal workflow beyond it.

## Decisions and Why They Matter

**Prop fabrication and finishing received a dedicated sustained context.**

This separated detailed fabrication execution from broader household, collection, and workshop operations.

**Individual fabrication jobs did not automatically become top-level ARCHIE workstreams.**

The RUN pattern allowed specific builds to retain focused execution context beneath the broader LAB domain.

**ARCHIE's September 4 persistence rule was therefore refined rather than abandoned.**

A sustained domain could receive its own workstream while individual jobs within that domain could remain subordinate units of work.

## Options Considered or Rejected

Keeping all detailed prop-fabrication activity in ARCHIE HQ was not used as the continuing structure.

Creating a new top-level ARCHIE workstream for every individual fabrication project was also avoided.

The LAB/RUN pattern provided a narrower hierarchy between those two extremes.

## Milestones Reached

**LAB — Prop Fabrication & Finishing** was established as a dedicated ARCHIE workstream.

A **RUN** pattern existed for individual fabrication work.

`RUN — Captain America Shield Finish` provides a recoverable example of that pattern.

ARCHIE therefore gained a more explicit distinction between:

- broad project operations;
- sustained specialist workstreams;
- and focused execution work within those workstreams.

## Current Project State

By the September 5 stopping point, ARCHIE's organizational model had advanced beyond its September 4 initialization.

ARCHIE HQ remained the general operations layer.

Persistent specialist domains could receive dedicated workstreams.

LAB provided that dedicated environment for prop fabrication and finishing.

Individual fabrication jobs could then be handled as RUNs without requiring each job to become another top-level project structure.

The surviving evidence does not establish a more detailed formal LAB/RUN specification on September 5.
