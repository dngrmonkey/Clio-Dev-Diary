## AJ's Objective

AJ continued formalizing the Iron Man helmet collection following the September 7 decision to avoid unnecessary physical duplication.

The objective was to make the **Iron Man Helmet Registry** useful as a continuing collection-management system rather than simply a list of armor Marks.

That required two things: consistent identification and naming of helmets already represented in the collection, and repeatable rules for selecting 3D models when additional helmet designs were needed.

## Work Completed

### Helmet Identity and Naming Conventions

Existing helmet identities were reviewed and normalized where the available evidence supported doing so.

The collection adopted or retained several practical identifiers:

- **WM-01** for the original War Machine representation.
- **WM-02** for Iron Patriot.
- **MDL** as a temporary placeholder for the Ironheart helmet whose exact Mark/model identity had not yet been established.

An earlier identification of one helmet as MK 42 was reconsidered, with the available evidence instead suggesting MK 3.

The important registry principle was that uncertain identity should remain visibly uncertain rather than being converted into false precision merely to complete the catalog.

### Duplicate-Aware Collection Development

The September 7 duplication strategy remained active.

The registry was intended to distinguish between canonical armor identities and the physical helmet designs actually worth adding to the collection.

This meant model acquisition should account for what was already represented rather than repeatedly selecting files for substantially equivalent helmet geometry.

Creator, model, and variant choices therefore became part of collection-management decisions rather than isolated download choices.

### 3D Model Acquisition Policy

A repeatable acquisition strategy was developed for cases where multiple printable versions of the same helmet were available.

The working policy favored:

- maintaining both **free and paid alternatives** when useful;
- choosing a viable lower-cost paid model rather than assuming the most expensive option was automatically preferable;
- retaining an additional paid alternative when it materially improved choice;
- when many free versions existed, prioritizing approximately the **two most-downloaded** credible options rather than collecting every available file;
- preferring established or well-used creators where popularity and download history provided useful evidence;
- maintaining creator consistency across related helmets when practical;
- maintaining compatible scale across the collection;
- allowing a full armor package to serve as a valid acquisition source when no suitable helmet-only package existed.

The policy was designed to provide useful alternatives without turning the library into an uncontrolled accumulation of near-identical files.

### Categorized Results

Model-search results were expected to be presented in meaningful categories rather than as an undifferentiated list.

Free, paid, alternate, and package-based options could therefore be compared according to their role in the collection.

This supported AJ's final selection while keeping the acquisition process consistent.

## Important Questions and Discussions

A major question was how much redundancy should exist at the **digital-file level** even when redundant physical printing was being avoided.

Keeping every available model would create unnecessary library growth.

Keeping only one file would eliminate useful alternatives and make the collection dependent on a single creator's interpretation.

The emerging answer was controlled redundancy: enough alternatives to preserve meaningful choice, but not indiscriminate accumulation.

Another issue was source consistency.

Using related models from the same creator could improve visual consistency, scaling, assembly expectations, and future collection coherence. Creator consistency therefore became a useful selection factor without becoming an absolute requirement.

The registry also needed to tolerate incomplete identification. A placeholder such as **MDL** was preferable to inventing a precise canonical designation unsupported by the evidence.

## Decisions and Why They Matter

**Helmet identities should not be forced when uncertain.**

Temporary identifiers can preserve operational usefulness without converting assumptions into registry facts.

**WM-01 and WM-02 provide stable War Machine-family identifiers.**

This distinguishes the original War Machine representation from Iron Patriot without relying solely on potentially ambiguous naming.

**Model acquisition should preserve useful alternatives without collecting everything.**

This limits library growth while retaining meaningful creator and price options.

**Free and paid sources can coexist.**

Price category alone does not determine which model deserves preservation.

**Download/popularity signals can help narrow large candidate pools.**

When many free models exist, the collection should favor a small number of established options rather than ingesting all available variants.

**Creator consistency and common scale are desirable collection properties.**

These improve coherence across related physical builds.

**Full armor packages are valid sources when necessary.**

The absence of a helmet-only release should not prevent acquisition when an appropriate helmet exists inside a larger package.

**Acquisition remains subordinate to duplicate control.**

A technically good model does not automatically justify acquisition or fabrication if the collection already represents substantially equivalent geometry.

## Options Considered or Rejected

Collecting every available version of a helmet was rejected as the default strategy.

Selecting only free models was rejected.

Selecting only paid models was rejected.

Assuming higher price meant better collection value was rejected.

Automatically treating creator folder structure or naming as definitive identity was avoided.

Forcing uncertain helmets into specific canonical Mark numbers was rejected.

Allowing different model sources to drift into incompatible scales without consideration was rejected.

## Problems, Surprises, or Course Corrections

One existing helmet identification required reconsideration: a helmet previously thought to represent MK 42 appeared more likely to represent MK 3.

Rather than silently replacing one assumption with another absolute claim, the registry work reinforced the need to distinguish confirmed identification from provisional identification.

An attempt was also made to extend the workflow through a library/agent installation mechanism.

That attempt failed with a **`library file not found`** condition.

The failed installation was not treated as an operational capability. No durable agent or library integration should be considered established from that attempt.

The collection-management rules therefore remained the reliable result of the work, independent of that failed extension attempt.

## Milestones Reached

The Iron Man Helmet Registry progressed from general duplicate-awareness into a more explicit operating system for collection decisions.

Stable identifiers were established for the War Machine/Iron Patriot representations.

A temporary identity mechanism was used for unresolved Ironheart classification.

An earlier helmet identification was reconsidered rather than preserved merely for consistency.

A free/paid acquisition strategy was defined.

Download/popularity signals, creator consistency, scale, alternate models, and full-armor-package sourcing became explicit acquisition considerations.

The collection now had a repeatable strategy for finding useful model alternatives without indiscriminately accumulating every available file.

## Current Project State

By the September 8 stopping point, the Iron Man helmet collection had three increasingly distinct layers:

**canonical identity** — what armor or character a helmet represents;

**digital representation** — which creator/model files are worth retaining as useful options;

**physical representation** — which designs justify actual fabrication and display.

These layers were related but no longer treated as interchangeable.

The registry and acquisition rules supported that separation while leaving AJ as the decision authority for ambiguous identification and final model selection.

## Unresolved Items

Some helmet identities remained provisional.

The Ironheart **MDL** designation remained a placeholder pending stronger identification.

The suspected MK 3 identification still required appropriate confidence before being treated as definitive.

The broader registry still required continued population as additional helmets and model sources were evaluated.

Creator consistency and scale compatibility would need to be checked during actual acquisitions rather than assumed from policy alone.

The attempted library/agent installation had not produced a working operational capability.

## Stopping Point and Next Action

September 8 ended with the **Iron Man Helmet Registry operating under a substantially clearer identification and acquisition policy**.

The collection now had rules for handling uncertainty, duplicate avoidance, free and paid alternatives, popularity signals, creator consistency, scale, and package-based acquisition.

The next work should apply these rules during continued collection development rather than immediately expanding the governance structure again.

The failed library/agent installation should remain recorded as unsuccessful unless later evidence establishes that the capability was repaired and made operational.
