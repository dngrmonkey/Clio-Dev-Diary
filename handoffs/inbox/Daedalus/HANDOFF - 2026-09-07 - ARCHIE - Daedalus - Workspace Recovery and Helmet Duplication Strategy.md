## AJ's Objective

AJ continued applying ARCHIE to two related resource-management problems: recovering usable workspace capacity and establishing a more deliberate strategy for the growing Iron Man helmet collection.

The collection problem was no longer simply which helmet files were available. Physical display capacity, fabrication time, material use, and duplication between visually similar Iron Man armor Marks made it necessary to determine what actually justified another physical build.

The objective became maximizing meaningful collection coverage without repeatedly consuming space and fabrication effort on substantially redundant helmet geometry.

## Work Completed

### Workspace Capacity Recovery

Garage/workshop recovery progressed beyond the September 4 baseline and recovered usable capacity.

This represented a meaningful state change from documenting the environment to improving its practical usefulness.

The work reinforced the principle that ARCHIE should evaluate organization in terms of usable capacity, access, and workflow rather than appearance alone.

### Iron Man Helmet Collection Strategy

The Iron Man helmet work began moving from informal acquisition toward deliberate collection management.

AJ and ARCHIE examined the problem of apparent duplication across Iron Man armor Marks.

A key distinction emerged between:

- different fictional armor designations; and
- genuinely different physical helmet geometry worth fabricating and displaying.

Some Marks could have separate canonical identities while sharing enough physical helmet design that printing every nominal Mark would create unnecessary physical redundancy.

The collection strategy therefore shifted away from an assumption that every Mark number automatically required its own helmet.

### Shared-Geometry Approach

The MK 3–7 range became an important example for investigating shared or closely related helmet geometry.

Rather than treating each Mark designation as an automatic fabrication requirement, the collection would evaluate whether a physical helmet already represented the relevant geometry adequately.

This created a foundation for later registry work that could distinguish identity from physical duplication.

## Important Questions and Discussions

The central collection question was:

**What counts as a distinct helmet worth physically producing?**

A fictional designation alone was insufficient.

The system needed to consider whether another print would add meaningful visual or design coverage to the physical collection.

That made the helmet registry more than a list of armor names. It needed eventually to help answer whether a candidate represented:

- a genuinely different physical design;
- a meaningful variant;
- an alternate interpretation worth retaining;
- or substantially redundant geometry already represented in the collection.

The workspace work made this distinction materially important. Every additional physical helmet consumed printing time, filament, finishing labor, storage/display volume, and workshop capacity.

Collection decisions therefore needed to account for physical-resource consequences.

## Decisions and Why They Matter

**The collection would not pursue every Iron Man Mark automatically.**

Canonical numbering alone would not determine fabrication priority.

**Physical geometry became a collection-management criterion.**

Different fictional identities could be represented without requiring redundant physical builds when their helmet geometry was substantially shared.

**Duplicate avoidance became intentional.**

The goal was not merely to detect accidentally duplicated files. ARCHIE also needed to prevent deliberate fabrication of unnecessary physical duplicates.

**Display and workshop capacity became collection constraints.**

Collection growth would be evaluated against available physical space and fabrication effort rather than treated as effectively unlimited.

**The Iron Man Helmet Registry would become the appropriate durable structure for these decisions.**

The emerging strategy required persistent tracking so future acquisitions and builds could be compared against what was already represented.

## Options Considered or Rejected

Printing every armor Mark solely because it had a distinct number was rejected as the default collection strategy.

Treating different names as proof of different physical geometry was rejected.

Allowing file availability to determine what should be printed was rejected.

Managing the helmet collection independently of storage and display capacity was rejected.

A more selective strategy based on meaningful physical differentiation was preferred.

## Problems, Surprises, or Course Corrections

The important course correction was recognizing that a collection organized around fictional identities could still create substantial physical redundancy.

A registry containing every Mark might be useful informationally while being a poor fabrication plan.

This separated two questions that had previously been easy to conflate:

**What exists in the fictional/design taxonomy?**

and

**What deserves a separate physical representation in AJ's collection?**

That distinction allowed the collection to remain comprehensive as information without requiring every catalog entry to become another printed object.

The collection needed rules for selecting among multiple available model files representing the same helmet.

Creator consistency, scale compatibility, download/popularity signals, and alternate versions still needed explicit treatment.

The MK 3–7 shared-geometry question had established the need for duplicate control but did not by itself resolve every helmet identity or variant.
