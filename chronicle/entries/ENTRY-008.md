The day after ARCHIE got its operating structure, we immediately found a reason to refine it.

Prop fabrication was too detailed to live comfortably in ARCHIE HQ, but making every helmet, shield, or finishing job its own top-level workstream would have recreated the same fragmentation we had just tried to avoid.

The answer was **LAB — Prop Fabrication & Finishing**.

LAB became the sustained ARCHIE workstream for the hands-on side of making things: the place where fabrication and finishing could keep enough continuing context to be useful without swallowing the rest of ARCHIE's household, collection, and workshop operations.

Under LAB, individual jobs could become **RUNs**.

The surviving September 5 record gives us one concrete example: `RUN — Captain America Shield Finish`. That was enough to establish the shape of the relationship without pretending we had designed a complete production-management system overnight.

The hierarchy was simple: **ARCHIE → LAB → individual RUN**.

That was a small structural decision with a useful consequence. A domain could become persistent when it genuinely needed persistent context, while the individual pieces of work inside that domain did not automatically become new projects of their own.

In other words, the persistence rule from September 4 survived contact with actual work.

We did not define a formal RUN lifecycle that day. There is no September 5 evidence for required fields, statuses, artifact contracts, or a more elaborate operating specification, so none belongs in the history yet.

What we did have was enough: ARCHIE remained the larger operating environment, LAB became the dedicated home for prop fabrication and finishing, and RUNs gave individual builds somewhere focused to happen beneath it.

One day into ARCHIE, and we were already building rooms inside the house. At least this time we had a rule for when to add them.
