By September 7, ARCHIE was dealing with the inevitable collision between collecting things and having somewhere to put them.

The garage and workshop had moved beyond the photographic baseline from September 4. Usable capacity had actually been recovered. That mattered because the point of the recovery effort had never been cosmetic tidiness; it was access, work surfaces, fabrication flow, and the ability to use the space for the things AJ wanted to build.

At the same time, the Iron Man helmet collection was creating its own capacity problem.

Up to this point, it was easy to think about the collection in terms of armor Marks: different number, different helmet, add it to the list. But fictional identity and physical geometry were not always the same thing. Two Marks could be canonically distinct while giving us very little reason to consume another print cycle, more filament, finishing labor, and display space on substantially the same helmet shape.

The MK 3–7 range became the useful test case.

That forced us to separate two questions that looked similar until physical space got involved: **What exists in the armor taxonomy?** and **What deserves a separate physical representation in AJ's collection?**

Those were not going to have identical answers.

AJ's collection could remain informationally comprehensive without requiring every cataloged Mark to become another object on a shelf. Physical geometry therefore became an explicit collection criterion. A new Mark number alone was not enough; another build needed to add meaningful visual or design coverage.

That also changed the purpose of the Iron Man Helmet Registry. It could not merely be a checklist of names. It needed to help distinguish identities, variants, genuinely different designs, and geometry already represented well enough that another physical build would mostly be duplication.

File availability was not going to make that decision for us either. The existence of a printable model did not create an obligation to print it. This may be obvious when written down. Anyone who has ever had access to a 3D printer understands why it occasionally needs to be written down.

By the end of September 7, the garage had more usable capacity and the helmet collection had a more deliberate relationship with that capacity.

The next problem was obvious: if the registry was going to guide real acquisition and build decisions, it needed consistent naming and rules for choosing among competing model files.

That was the work waiting for September 8.
