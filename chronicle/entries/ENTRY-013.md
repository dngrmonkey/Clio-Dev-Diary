With the September 12 recovery point verified, ARCHIE finally had permission to stop studying the Actus collection from a safe distance and start organizing it in production.

Headgear went first.

The rules did not loosen just because we had reached the exciting part. Original sources stayed untouched. The recovery point stayed untouched. Complete packages—not isolated duplicate files—remained the unit of judgment. Different creators, revisions, alternates, instructions, images, and supporting material stayed with their packages. Ambiguity still went to review instead of being solved by optimism.

The production process now had to do the slow work the collection-wide fingerprint report could not do: inspect source packages, compare extracted copies, reconcile ZIP payloads, preserve meaningful differences, copy a verified package into the clean library, hash-check the result, and record where it came from.

ZIP files made that especially important. A matching archive could be documented without creating another browsing copy. A ZIP-only helmet could become a usable package after verification. A materially different archive needed to survive as its own thing.

And some archives simply refused to fit neatly into Headgear.

A Batman/Predator package with generic component names stayed in review because the evidence did not establish its identity well enough. A Darth Maul/Iron Man archive also needed more assessment. A complete Iron Man suit package contained chest, limb, helmet, and other components, so it was deferred intact to Armor instead of being torn apart just to make the Headgear numbers look better.

That was exactly the behavior we wanted. The category boundary was not permission to damage a package that belonged to a later phase.

By September 14, production had reached the directly documented **Headgear batch 025**. That batch included a group of Halo/Star Wars crossover helmets—Heavy Mando Spartan, Armorer Spartan Helmet, Phase 2 Spartan Helmet, Galactic Spartan, and Phase 1 Spartan Helmet. Because they were crossovers, the metadata could acknowledge both universes instead of forcing them into one.

ZIP processing had separately reached **ZIP helmets 005**, including recognized candidates such as Medieval Iron Man, What If Giant Man Bill Foster, Mando Iron Man, Iron Man Shotgun, and Dragon Slayer IronMan.

Those batch numbers were progress markers, not proof that Headgear was finished.

ARCHIE made that explicit with **`Interim audit checkpoint 20260914-1140`**. The checkpoint was actually created, its audit records were SHA-256 verified, and its own manifest said the phase was incomplete. Coverage remained unfinished. Research and review remained. The final catalog remained. Armor remained deferred.

That distinction became even more important later in the day when AJ narrowed the scope.

The instruction was now to **finish Helmet/Headgear, create the required final records and recovery state, hard-save it, and stop. Do not begin Armor.**

So September 14 did not end with a triumphant "Headgear complete." It ended with substantial production work safely preserved at an interim checkpoint and a much clearer definition of what completion would require.

The remaining work included full candidate accounting, more ZIP reconciliation, exclusions and Armor deferrals, unresolved-review items, final provenance and verification records, a dated Headgear catalog, coverage assessment, resume instructions, a verified incremental recovery backup, and the final hard-save.

Character grouping was still future work too. AJ had identified the eventual **Category → Universe → Character → Individual Design** structure, but no character regrouping happened on September 14.

And Armor definitely did not happen.

Sometimes the important milestone is knowing exactly where to stop.
